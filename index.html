magenais.decision-score · v1.0.0

Explainable multi-criteria decision intelligence.

Ranks options against weighted criteria, then tells you how much to trust the ranking: a Decision Stability Index for how robust it is, and a Decision Flip Point for exactly what would change it.

What it does

Given a set of options scored against weighted criteria, DecisionScore normalizes the scores, computes a weighted ranking, then runs two sensitivity checks: how often the top choice survives random perturbation of the weights (DSI), and how large a weight change would be needed to flip it (DFP).

It is a research-oriented model — the ranking method itself (normalized weighted sum) is a well-known technique from multi-criteria decision analysis; DSI and DFP are this model's own framing of sensitivity on top of it, not a claim of a new ranking algorithm. See docs/algorithm.md for the full method.

Worked example

Output of examples/basic-usage.mjs, run with a fixed seed — reproducible byte-for-byte.

RankOptionScore
1Vendor B0.667
2Vendor A0.422
3Vendor C0.333
 
Decision Stability Index (DSI): 1.00

Decision Flip Points:
Quality: a 70.0% decrease would flip the top choice to "vendor-c"
Price: a 105.0% increase would flip the top choice to "vendor-c"
Support: no flip found within the tested range

This is a sensitivity estimate under tested assumptions. It is not a guarantee.

Limitations

  • Assumes criteria are roughly commensurable once normalized — it doesn't model strong non-linear trade-offs.
  • constraints on the input is documentation-only in v1.0.0, not numerically enforced.
  • DSI/DFP are estimates over a tested perturbation range, not an exhaustive or formally-proven bound.
